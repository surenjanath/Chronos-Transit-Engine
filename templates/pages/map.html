Rings are true distances from the Sun, so a star above or below the drawing plane appears inside its ring. Orbits are drawn to scale in size, seen from above. A dot marks where a planet is on its orbit today: for the Solar System from each planet’s mean motion, for other stars by counting orbits since a measured transit, when the planet stood on the line from its star to the Sun. A planet gets no dot when it has no measured transit time, or when its period is not known well enough to say where it is by now. Each planet’s table gives the measured tilt of its orbit to our line of sight (90° is edge-on); which way the orbit is turned around that line has not been measured for any of them, so the drawing shows every orbit from above. The Oort cloud and the edge of the Galaxy’s disc are approximate.
